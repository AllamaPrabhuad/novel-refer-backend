Baby Wipes Fabric Explained: GSM, Spunlace and Plant-Based Substrates

What GSM means for baby wipes, how spunlace nonwovens are made, viscose vs polyester blends vs plant-based fibres, and how to pick the right wipe fabric for your brand.

When parents say a wipe feels "thin", "rough" or "luxurious", they're talking about the fabric — the substrate. It's one of the biggest drivers of both perceived quality and cost, so it's worth understanding before you choose.

What is spunlace?

Most baby wipes use spunlace (hydroentangled) nonwoven fabric. Instead of being woven or glued, fibres are laid into a web and entangled by fine high-pressure water jets. The result is soft, strong and absorbent, with no chemical binders — well suited to products that touch baby skin.

What does GSM mean?

GSM is grams per square metre — the fabric's weight. Higher GSM generally means a thicker, stronger, more cushioned wipe. It also means more fibre per wipe, which raises cost.

GSM bandFeelTypical positioning
Lighter (around 40–45)Thin, flexibleValue packs, hand & face wipes
Mid (around 45–55)Soft, everyday strengthMainstream baby wipes
Heavier (55–60+)Thick, plushPremium and newborn ranges

GSM isn't the whole story. Fibre blend, the embossed pattern and the lotion all change how thick a wipe feels, so always judge samples in hand.

Choosing the fibre blend

Viscose / polyester blends

Polyester adds strength and keeps costs down. Viscose — a cellulose fibre made from wood pulp — adds softness and absorbency. Blends are the workhorse of the category.

100% viscose

Softer and more absorbent, and free of synthetic polymer fibres — often chosen for premium or "plant-based" positioning.

Other plant-based fibres

Fibres such as lyocell, or cotton-rich blends, support natural positioning. Availability and cost vary, so sample early.

A note on claims: words like "plastic-free", "biodegradable" or "flushable" must match the exact substrate — and in some cases test data. Confirm the evidence with your manufacturer before they go on pack.

How the lotion interacts with fabric

Different substrates hold lotion differently. A heavier, more absorbent fabric may need more lotion to feel equally moist. That's why fabric and lotion should be specified together, and why the lid and seal matter for keeping the last wipe as moist as the first.

How to choose

  1. Start from your positioning: value, mainstream or premium.
  2. Sample two or three GSM levels in your preferred blend.
  3. Test them the way parents will — on a messy change, not just by touch.
  4. Check cost per pack at your target count.
  5. Lock the specification with tolerances so every batch matches.
