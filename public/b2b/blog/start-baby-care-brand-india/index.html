How to Start Your Own Baby Care Brand in India: A Private Label Guide

A step-by-step guide to launching your own baby care brand in India with a private label manufacturer — positioning, range, samples, packaging, compliance, costing and launch.

India's baby care shelf has changed. Parents now compare ingredients, read reviews and buy from marketplaces, quick-commerce apps and D2C sites as readily as from the corner chemist. That shift has opened the door for new brands — and almost none of them own a factory. They work with a private label manufacturer that formulates, fills and packs products under the brand's name.

This guide walks through how to go from idea to first shipment.

1. Decide who your brand is for

"Baby care" is broad. Successful brands start narrow and clear:

  • Positioning: natural and plant-based, dermatologist-led, newborn-sensitive, value-for-money, premium gifting?
  • Customer: first-time parents in metros, value-conscious families, hospitals and maternity homes, export markets?
  • Channel: your own website, marketplaces, quick-commerce, pharmacies, modern trade — each shapes pack sizes and pricing.

Write your brand promise in one sentence. Every formula, fragrance and pack decision should support it.

2. Build a focused launch range

Most brands launch with a hero product plus two or three supporting SKUs. Baby wipes are a common hero: they are bought often, so they bring repeat customers. A typical starter range might be:

  • Baby wet wipes (for example a 72- or 80-count pack, plus a travel pack)
  • A tear-free shampoo or head-to-toe wash
  • A baby lotion or massage oil
  • Optionally, a baby laundry detergent to own more of the nursery

Working with a manufacturer that makes all of these saves you managing several suppliers, quality standards and delivery schedules.

3. Choose the right manufacturing partner

Look for a partner whose capacity comfortably exceeds your three-year plan, not just your first order. Ask about plants and machines, in-house formulation, quality control, pack formats, minimum order quantities and confidentiality. 4. Sample, compare and lock specifications

Request samples of the bases and substrates that match your positioning. Compare them side by side: wipe thickness and moisture, shampoo foam and fragrance, lotion texture and absorption. Once you decide, the manufacturer turns your choices into a written specification — the reference every batch is checked against.

5. Packaging and artwork

Packaging is where your brand lives on the shelf. Decide formats (flip-top wipes lid or sticker, pump or flip-cap bottle, tube or jar), sizes and colours. Then brief your designer with the exact pack dimensions and label areas from your manufacturer.

Plan your on-pack claims early. Claims like "tear-free", "dermatologically tested" or "plant-based" need supporting evidence, and the testing behind them takes time.

6. Understand compliance

In India, cosmetics are regulated under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and the Cosmetics Rules, 2020. Packaged goods must also meet the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules — MRP, net quantity, manufacturer and marketer details, customer care contact and more. Your manufacturer's licences cover manufacturing. You remain responsible as the brand owner for your labels and claims, and for registering your trademark.

Requirements change and depend on the product, so confirm the current rules for your category with a regulatory consultant before you print.

7. Cost your product properly

Your landed cost per unit includes formulation, packaging components, printing, filling, testing, freight and warehousing. Then build up to MRP through your channel margins: marketplace commissions, distributor and retailer margins, returns, advertising and GST. Many first-time brands under-price because they leave out performance-marketing costs — model them from day one.

8. Plan your first run and your second

Minimum order quantities are usually driven by printed packaging (film rolls, labels, cartons) and batch sizes. Order enough to launch and learn, but agree a reorder process before you go live. A rolling three-month forecast shared with your manufacturer helps you avoid stock-outs, which can sink a marketplace listing's ranking.

9. Launch, listen and expand

Use your first months to read reviews and repeat-purchase data. Then add the next SKUs your customers ask for — often a fragrance-free variant, a value multipack or a gift set.

The short version

  1. Define a clear positioning and channel.
  2. Launch a focused range around a hero product.
  3. Choose a manufacturer that can scale with you.
  4. Sample, decide and lock specifications.
  5. Design packaging and plan claims early.
  6. Get compliance right before you print.
  7. Cost every rupee, including marketing.
  8. Forecast reorders from day one.
