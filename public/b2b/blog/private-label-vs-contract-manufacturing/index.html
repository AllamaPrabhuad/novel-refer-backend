Private Label vs Contract Manufacturing vs White Label: What's the Difference?

Private label, contract manufacturing and white label explained for baby care brands — who owns the formula, how much you can customise, MOQs, speed and cost.

If you're planning a baby care brand you will hear three terms used — often interchangeably: white label, private label and contract manufacturing. They overlap, but the differences matter for how unique your product is, who owns the formula and how fast you can launch.

White label: a ready product with your name on it

The manufacturer has an existing product — formula, and often packaging too — and sells it to many brands, each with its own label. You choose from a catalogue and add your branding.

  • Pros: fastest route to market, lowest development cost, often lower minimum quantities.
  • Cons: other brands may sell essentially the same product, so it is harder to stand out on ingredients.

Private label: proven bases, customised for you

You start from the manufacturer's tested base formulas and customise them — fragrance, extracts, free-from profile, texture, pack format and design — to create a product sold only under your brand.

  • Pros: a differentiated product without starting from zero; development risk is lower because the base is already proven.
  • Cons: takes longer than white label, and the underlying base usually remains the manufacturer's know-how.

Contract manufacturing: your formula, their factory

You bring the formula — developed by you or your formulator — and the manufacturer produces it to your specification under a confidentiality agreement.

  • Pros: maximum control and ownership of the formula, so it's easier to move or dual-source later.
  • Cons: you carry the development cost and the responsibility for testing it.

Which is right for a baby care brand?

Most first-time baby brands do best with private label. Baby products face tough scrutiny from parents, so starting from bases with a track record lowers risk, while customisation lets you build a real point of difference.

As you grow, you can move hero products to your own proprietary formulas under contract manufacturing — ideally with the same partner, so specifications, quality systems and supply stay consistent.

Questions to settle in writing

  • Who owns the formula and the specifications?
  • Who owns the artwork and print plates or cylinders?
  • What confidentiality (NDA) terms apply?
  • Which tests are included, and who pays for claim-support testing?
  • How are price changes for raw materials handled?

Clear answers up front make for a long, low-drama manufacturing relationship.
